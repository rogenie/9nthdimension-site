**When prayer feels dry, keep praying, but simplify: shorter prayers, honest words and one steady time each day.** Dryness is a normal part of spiritual growth, especially on the Healing rung, and it is often a sign that your faith is moving from feelings toward trust.

## Why prayer feels dry

Early in a spiritual journey, prayer often comes with warm feelings. Over time those feelings fade, and many people assume something has gone wrong. Usually nothing has. Common reasons include:

- **Your faith is maturing.** Christian writers have long described seasons of dryness as a call to trust God rather than the feeling of God.
- **You are tired or stretched thin.** Stress, grief and poor sleep flatten every kind of feeling, including spiritual ones.
- **Something unspoken is in the way.** A resentment, disappointment or question you have not said out loud can make prayer feel like small talk.
- **Your routine has gone stale.** Praying the same words in the same way for months can turn into recitation.

## Five ways to keep praying through a dry season

1. **Pray shorter.** One honest sentence a day keeps the connection open when longer prayers feel empty.
2. **Say the hard thing.** Tell God exactly how it feels: "I don't feel you, and I'm disappointed." The Psalms are full of prayers like this, such as Psalm 13, which opens by asking how long God will seem to hide.
3. **Borrow words.** Pray a psalm or the Lord's Prayer slowly. Borrowed words carry you when your own run out.
4. **Change the setting.** Pray on a walk, by a window or in your car before work. A new place can loosen an old rut.
5. **Keep a tiny record.** Note one line each day about what you prayed. Looking back after a few weeks often shows answers you missed.

## What dryness is preparing you for

People who keep praying through dry seasons often describe a quieter, steadier faith on the other side, one that does not rise and fall with their mood. That steadiness is the mark of Rung 5 and Rung 6. Dryness is not the ladder breaking; it is often the next rung appearing.
