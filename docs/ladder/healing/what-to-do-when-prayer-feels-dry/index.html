Healing, rung IV

What to do when prayer feels dry

By the 9nth Dimension team · Published November 11, 2026 · Last updated November 11, 2026

When prayer feels dry, keep praying, but simplify: shorter prayers, honest words and one steady time each day. Dryness is a normal part of spiritual growth, especially on the Healing rung, and it is often a sign that your faith is moving from feelings toward trust.

Why prayer feels dry

Early in a spiritual journey, prayer often comes with warm feelings. Over time those feelings fade, and many people assume something has gone wrong. Usually nothing has. Common reasons include:

  • Your faith is maturing. Christian writers have long described seasons of dryness as a call to trust God rather than the feeling of God.
  • You are tired or stretched thin. Stress, grief and poor sleep flatten every kind of feeling, including spiritual ones.
  • Something unspoken is in the way. A resentment, disappointment or question you have not said out loud can make prayer feel like small talk.
  • Your routine has gone stale. Praying the same words in the same way for months can turn into recitation.

Does the Bible talk about seasons when God feels silent?

Yes, often and honestly. Scripture does not pretend that faithful people always feel close to God.

  • Psalm 22 opens with the cry "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" and goes on to describe calling out by day and night without an answer (Psalm 22:1–2). Jesus prayed its first line from the cross (Matthew 27:46).
  • Psalm 42 compares the soul to a deer thirsting for water, and twice the writer asks his own soul why it is so downcast, then tells himself to put his hope in God (Psalm 42:1, 5, 11).
  • Elijah expected to meet God in wind, earthquake and fire, but God came in a quiet whisper instead (1 Kings 19:11–12).

These passages show two things. Feeling far from God is part of real faith, not a failure of it. And the people who wrote them kept speaking to God while they felt that way.

Is it spiritual dryness or something more?

Spiritual dryness usually affects prayer and worship while the rest of life carries on reasonably well. You still enjoy food, friends and work; prayer just feels flat.

If the flatness has spread everywhere, with low mood most days, trouble sleeping, losing interest in things you used to enjoy, or feeling hopeless, it may be depression rather than, or as well as, a spiritual season. That deserves real care. Please talk to a doctor or licensed counsellor, and to a pastor you trust. Getting help is not a lack of faith; it is one of the ways God cares for you.

Five ways to keep praying through a dry season

  1. Pray shorter. One honest sentence a day keeps the connection open when longer prayers feel empty.
  2. Say the hard thing. Tell God exactly how it feels: "I don't feel you, and I'm disappointed." The Psalms are full of prayers like this, such as Psalm 13, which opens by asking how long God will seem to hide.
  3. Borrow words. Pray a psalm or the Lord's Prayer slowly. Borrowed words carry you when your own run out.
  4. Change the setting. Pray on a walk, by a window or in your car before work. A new place can loosen an old rut.
  5. Keep a tiny record. Note one line each day about what you prayed. Looking back after a few weeks often shows answers you missed.

Today's practice: the one-line honest prayer. Tonight, write one sentence to God that says exactly how prayer feels right now. Then read Psalm 13 slowly. Notice how the writer moves from complaint to trust in six short verses, without pretending to feel better first.

What dryness is preparing you for

People who keep praying through dry seasons often describe a quieter, steadier faith on the other side, one that does not rise and fall with their mood. That steadiness is the mark of Rung 5 and Rung 6. Dryness is not the ladder breaking; it is often the next rung appearing.

If you are in a dry season right now, you do not need to fix it today. Pray one honest line, read one psalm, and come back tomorrow. Faithfulness in small things is how every rung is climbed.

Frequently asked questions

Does dry prayer mean God has left me?

No. Seasons of dryness are a common part of spiritual growth, described by believers for centuries. Feeling distant is a feeling, not a measure of how near God actually is.

How long does spiritual dryness last?

It varies from days to months. Dryness tends to lift as you keep simple, honest habits rather than waiting to feel close again before you pray.

Should I stop praying until I feel something?

It is usually better to keep praying, but more simply. Short, honest prayers kept up during a dry season often lead to a deeper, steadier faith afterwards.

When should I talk to someone about it?

If dryness comes with ongoing low mood, poor sleep or loss of interest in daily life, talk to a pastor and a doctor or counsellor. Spiritual dryness and depression can feel alike and deserve care.
